# Counsel brief: the investor side

*For a fixed-fee review, to follow the earlier brief (`docs/legal/counsel-brief.md`). Questions marked **B** block opening the first switch; the rest can follow the first supervised run. Facts are checked against the code; positions are drafts.*

## About Anker, for this purpose
Anker is a venture platform operated from the EU. Until now its users were founders and fund managers. The **investor side** adds a third kind of user: a person (or a person acting for a company, trust or family office) who signs up to find venture funds, asks funds to talk, and goes through a fund's onboarding. Anker does not take money, sign subscriptions on anyone's behalf, give advice, or make any investment decision. A fund decides whom it talks to and whom it accepts; Anker provides the register, the conversation and the paperwork flow.

What a person can do, in order: (1) create an account after proving their email with a code; (2) describe themselves and what they look for, for one or several "entities" (themselves, a company, a trust, a family office), optionally with a team; (3) see funds that a fund manager chose to list, ranked by how well each fund's stated focus matches what the investor says they look for, with an eligibility check against the fund's own rules; (4) ask a fund to talk ("an expression of interest, not an offer") and choose what to share; (5) once the fund accepts, exchange messages and files, accept the fund's own NDA, receive documents the fund shares, and arrange meetings; (6) if the fund invites them to onboard, the fund's investor record is created and the fund's own verification and subscription flow (docs 60) takes over; (7) keep identity documents once in a "passport" and share a copy with a fund, with consent, per fund; (8) optionally let funds find a limited card and invite them; (9) download all their data, or erase their account.

## The questions

### A. Roles, bases, notice (cross-cutting)
1. **B. Anker's role.** We propose: Anker is a **controller** of investor accounts, profiles, entities, teams and the passport store; a **fund** is a controller of what it receives and holds about an investor once a request is made (the shared copy, the conversation, the documents it asked for, its onboarding record); Anker acts as the fund's **processor** for the fund's own onboarding records (doc 60) as already assumed for LPs. Is that split right, is any part joint controllership, and what must the fund-facing terms and the DPA say? *Unlocks: the privacy notice, the fund terms, everything below.*
2. **B. Lawful bases.** Proposed: account and profile, performance of the contract; requests, conversations, NDA and meetings, steps at the person's request before a contract; passport storage, consent (per document, withdrawable) with performance of the contract as a fallback counsel may prefer; sharing a passport copy with one fund, consent; discoverability, consent; reminders and notices, legitimate interest or contract. Please confirm or replace each. *Unlocks: the notice (09).*
3. **B. The investor privacy notice.** A draft Article 13 notice is in `09`. The sign-up checkbox links to the platform's general terms and privacy notice (`/terms`, `/privacy`); that notice says nothing about investor-specific processing yet. Should there be a separate investor notice and investor terms, and what must they contain? *Unlocks: the sign-up wording.*

### B. Account and sign-up (01)
4. **B. Terms for investors.** Is the general terms acceptance enough, or do investors need their own terms (no advice, no offer, honesty of self-declarations, acceptable use, fund-specific terms apply to the fund relationship)? 
5. **Capacity and age.** Anker does not verify age or capacity. Is a statement needed, and for individuals only?
6. **Email verification.** An account does not exist until a six-digit code sent to the address is entered (10 minutes, three tries; limits per address and per network). Pending sign-ups are deleted after a day. Is this sufficient identification for what the account can do, which is expressing interest and receiving documents a fund chooses to share?
7. **Self-declared category.** Investors may state they are professional, semi-professional, other or unsure. It is always shown as "self-declared by the investor". Does recording this create any duty for Anker (classification, appropriateness, documentation), or exposure to being seen as classifying investors?

### C. Listing and fit (02)
8. **B. Is listing a fund "marketing"?** A fund manager may list the fund: a fixed summary from its own profile (name, manager, description, strategy, stages, sectors, geographies, size, minimum commitment, term, status, "who may invest") shown only to registered investors, with eligibility rules (categories, allowed and excluded countries, minimum cheque, professional only). Does this amount to marketing of an AIF or an offer under AIFMD or national private-placement rules (Germany: KAGB), or to investment or contract brokerage by Anker (KWG), and what must Anker require of fund managers (jurisdiction restrictions, a warranty that they may market to those investors, takedown)? Is the "who may invest" rule set enough to keep a fund out of jurisdictions it has not registered for?
9. **The ranking.** Funds are ranked by how well their stated focus matches what the investor says they look for (sectors, stage, geography, cheque size, horizon); eligibility is shown separately; no performance, no quality judgement, no AI. The page says "This is not advice and not a recommendation; check each fund yourself." Is a ranking of this kind a personal recommendation under MiFID II or similar, and is the wording enough? 
10. **Strict eligibility.** By default an investor who does not meet a fund's stated rules can still send a request, which the fund sees flagged. A fund can switch on **strict mode**, which refuses such a request. Should strict mode be the default, or mandatory for professional-only funds?
11. **Accuracy of what funds say.** Anker shows what the fund manager entered. Who is responsible, and what notice-and-takedown route is needed?

### D. Requests, conversations, NDA, files (03)
12. **The request statement.** "This is an expression of interest. It is not an offer, a subscription or an obligation. The fund sees what you choose to share below, and your email address only if it accepts." Sufficient?
13. **Conversations.** Anker stores the messages and attachments between a fund and an investor, encrypted for files (messages are plain text in the database). Who may read them: the two sides, and technically platform operators with database access. Is a confidentiality statement or a staff-access policy needed? 
14. **The NDA.** The fund pastes its own NDA text; the investor accepts by clicking; Anker stores the text, its hash, the time, the IP address and a hash of the browser string. Anker is not a party and does not review the text. Is click acceptance adequate for the funds' purposes (eIDAS simple electronic signature, evidence), and is the disclaimer wording right?
15. **Attachments.** Up to three PDF, JPEG or PNG files of 4 MB; the type is read from the content; PDFs with scripts, launch actions, embedded files, rich media or disguised names are refused. **There is no virus scanner.** Is that acceptable for launch, or must scanning precede it?
16. **Contact details.** The investor's email address reaches the fund only when the fund accepts (or the investor accepts the fund's invitation). Covered by the request statement?

### E. The passport (04)
17. **B. Identity documents.** Investors can store identity documents (ID, proof of address, source of funds; for entities, certificate of incorporation, ownership declaration) and share a copy with one fund that is onboarding them. Is storing them at all permissible and proportionate, on what basis, and as controller or processor? Are ID documents special-category data here? We understand that copies of German identity cards are restricted (marking as copy, purpose limits): what must Anker do, or refuse to do?
18. **The consent wording** for sharing, and the take-back rule: an unseen copy is deleted; a copy the fund has already checked stays with the fund for its legal records and the investor is told to ask the fund to remove it. Is that the right line?
19. **B. Where the files are kept.** Files are encrypted by the application (AES-256-GCM) in the database. The database is hosted by Neon in **AWS us-east-1 (US East, N. Virginia)**: read from the connection address on 2026-10-23, so confirmed. Hosting (Vercel) is recorded as US (inferred). Sign-in (Supabase) and email delivery (Resend) run in **the providers' default regions, which were not chosen deliberately**; the actual region of each is to be read from the provider's dashboard (most likely US East for both, not verified). Does storing EU residents' identity documents there, and sending them sign-in and email through default-region providers, need a different region, SCCs or another measure before launch? *Founder note: moving the database region, or storing passport files in EU storage, is possible but a project.*
20. **Key management.** One application key encrypts all files; there is **no rotation procedure yet**. Is that acceptable as a technical measure for launch?
21. **Retention.** See `08`. Are the proposed periods right? Anti-money-laundering retention belongs to the fund.

### F. Discoverability (05)
22. **Purpose and basis.** Funds see a card (kind of entity, country, self-declared category, cheque range, sectors, stages, regions, horizon; never name, address, team) for opted-in entities that meet the fund's rules, and can invite them to connect; nothing else is shared unless the investor accepts. Consent basis? Is the invitation (an in-app item and a content-free email "a fund would like to connect") direct marketing needing its own consent (UWG section 7)? Is the owner's switch, with its explanation, valid consent?
23. **Re-identification.** Cards are not shown until at least 10 entities have opted in (proposed). Is a card with kind, country and cheque range identifying in small populations, and is the threshold right?
24. **Automated ranking.** Cards are ranked by fit. Any Article 22 concern (no decision with legal effect is made)? 

### G. Rights, retention, security (06)
25. **Rights.** Access and portability: one file of everything held (profile, entities, requests, conversations, meetings, passport records, activity; not file contents). Erasure: removes the person and what is theirs, anonymises what funds keep (their relationship record, their own messages, onboarding records), deletes the sign-in. Is the split right, and is anonymisation an acceptable form of erasure for what funds must keep?
26. **DPIA.** An outline is in `06`. Is one required before launch, and who signs it?
27. **Breach.** A draft runbook is in `06`. Is it adequate?
28. **Sub-processors.** The register lists Neon, Vercel, Supabase, Resend, Stripe, Telegram and three AI providers. The investor side sends no investor data to AI providers (the ranking is deterministic). Please confirm the register is complete for this use, and the DPAs are in place.
