# 73. Build plan: counsel readiness for the investor side

Status: **built 2026-10-21; migration applied to production; see section 8.** Plan as written: Docs 64 to 72 built the investor side and left it closed behind `investor_signup`, `investor_discoverability` and `listed_for_lps` until counsel has read the wording. Counsel cannot review a product they cannot see, and some of what counsel will ask for does not exist yet. This document specifies what to build so that the review is possible, cheap and final: the wording in one place, the retention and the data-subject rights actually implemented, consent recorded by version, and a counsel pack that is generated from the same source as the product so it cannot drift.

## 1. One source for every sentence counsel must read
`lib/investor/legal.ts` holds each user-facing statement of legal weight, with an id, who sees it, where, and the text, under one `LEGAL_VERSION`. The screens import their wording from it; nothing legal is typed into a component. A test pins a hash of all the texts: changing a sentence fails the test until the change is made on purpose, the version is bumped, and the pack regenerated. The statements: account sign-up consent; the request statement ("an expression of interest, not an offer"); the fit-ranking disclaimer; what investors see of a listed fund and what a fund manager is told before listing; the NDA notices (investor and fund sides); the passport consent, share and take-back outcomes; the discoverability statement (owner and fund sides); the attachment warning; the erasure and export statements.

## 2. A pack generated from it
`scripts/legal/gen-investor-pack.ts` writes `docs/legal/investor-side/07-wording-as-shipped.md` from the registry; a test fails if the file on disk differs from what the registry generates. The other pack documents (01 to 06 and the brief) describe the facts and put the questions; they quote nothing by hand.

## 3. Retention that runs
Today the retention positions are statements. This builds a daily job (`/api/cron/investor-retention`) with a stated policy in `lib/investor/retention.ts`, every period marked **proposed, for counsel to confirm**, and a dry-run mode. Covered: expired sign-up and claim codes; answered, revoked and expired team invitations; unanswered discoverability invitations (expire after 30 days) and the cards kept on answered ones; passport files of entities archived more than 30 days; superseded passport records with no share history; message attachments of conversations closed more than a year; the entity activity log after three years. What is **not** removed by the job, by design: a fund's relationship records and its own messages, and anything a fund has already checked (onboarding records): those are the fund's, under its own retention.

## 4. The investor's rights, implemented
- **Access and portability:** `GET /api/investor/export` returns everything held about the person as one JSON file: profile, entities with their role, team and invitations, requests with the copy each fund received, the conversations of their entities, meetings, the passport's records (not the files; they download their own files one by one), shares and the activity log.
- **Erasure:** `POST /api/investor/erase` with a typed confirmation. It takes back unseen passport copies, wipes passport files, removes team, invitations, activity log, discoverability cards and the profile; withdraws open requests; **anonymises** what funds keep (the entity becomes "Erased investor", the shared copy keeps only the kind of entity, the investor's own messages are replaced by a removal notice and their attachments deleted, the NDA acceptance record is kept without address or browser data); deletes the sign-in account when the person has no workspace. A person who only belongs to someone else's team just leaves it. The outcome says what funds keep and why.
- Both are on the investor's profile page under "Your data", and both are audit-logged without personal content.

**A hazard found while testing this, and fixed:** the first investor migration made an entity's owner a foreign key to the profile with `ON DELETE CASCADE`, and a fund's relationship records hang off the entity with `ON DELETE CASCADE`. Deleting a profile would therefore have deleted the entities and, through them, the records funds are entitled to keep. The key is dropped; erasure anonymises the entity and removes the profile explicitly. A test pins that the funds' rows survive.

## 5. Consent recorded by version
Additive columns: the version of the passport consent on each share, the version of the discoverability statement on each entity that opts in, and the version of the request statement on each request. Sign-up already records the terms version and time.

## 6. The counsel pack (`docs/legal/investor-side/`)
A cover brief with numbered questions and the decision each unlocks; one document per area (account and sign-up; listing and eligibility; requests, talks, NDA and attachments; the passport; discoverability; data map, processors, retention and rights); the generated wording. Each says what the product does (verified against code and tests), what it does not do, what is proposed, and the exact question for counsel. A combined document is produced for sending. Nothing in the pack is legal advice or a legal position: positions are drafts for counsel to accept, change or reject, as in the existing `docs/legal` drafts.

## 7. Tests
The registry hash and the generated pack match; screens import their wording (spot-checked); retention: each rule removes what it should and leaves what it must keep, dry-run changes nothing, a second run does nothing; export carries the person's data and none of another person's, none of the files' bytes; erasure: the unseen copy is deleted and a checked one retained, the entity is anonymised not deleted, funds keep their side with names removed, the person's messages and files go, open requests withdrawn, a team member's erasure only removes their membership, a confirmation is required, nothing of another person is touched; the consent versions are recorded; the registry classes the job's tables correctly.

## 8. What was built, and what was not checked (2026-10-21)
- `lib/investor/legal.ts` (every sentence of legal weight and the emails; pure, so client components use it), `components/investor/legal-text.tsx`, the screens and emails now take their wording from it; `lib/investor/retention-policy.ts` and `retention.ts` with `/api/cron/investor-retention` (daily 03:40 UTC, dry-run `?dry=1`); `lib/investor/rights.ts` with `GET /api/investor/export`, `POST /api/investor/erase` and a "Your data" section on the investor profile; consent versions on shares, opt-ins and requests; **two controls counsel is likely to ask for:** strict eligibility mode (a fund refuses requests that clearly fail its rules) and a pool-size floor of 10 before any discoverability card is shown. Migration `2026-10-21-investor-legal.sql` applied to production.
- The pack: `docs/legal/investor-side/` (README, brief, six area documents, the generated wording and retention, a draft investor privacy notice) and a combined Word document. `07` and `08` are generated by `npx tsx scripts/legal/gen-investor-pack.ts`; tests fail if they differ from the code, and the wording is pinned by hash.
- Tested: 17 new tests on top of the earlier ones (registry pinned and pack in sync; screens use the registry; emails keep their promises; export carries one person's data and no files; erasure anonymises not deletes, takes back unseen copies, keeps checked ones and says so, spares a teammate and a workspace sign-in; every retention rule removes what it should and a second run does nothing; strict mode; pool floor).
- **Found and fixed while testing:** deleting a profile would have cascaded through the entity to the relationship records funds are entitled to keep (section 4).
- **Not checked:** the "Your data" section, the legal-text rendering and the strict-mode checkbox in a browser; a real erasure against production (there are no investors yet); the cron on Vercel. The retention numbers are proposals. The privacy notice and the investor terms are drafts; the pack is not legal advice.
