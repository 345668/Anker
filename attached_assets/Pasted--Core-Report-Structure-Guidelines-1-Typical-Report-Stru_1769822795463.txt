📘 Core Report Structure Guidelines
1) Typical Report Structure — Glasgow Caledonian University
Outlines the common sections in a structured report, including Executive Summary, Table of Contents, numbered headings, Conclusions, Recommendations, References, and Appendices.
Key takeaways:
Executive Summary should provide an overview of aims, findings, conclusions, and recommendations.
Main body divided into numbered headings and sub-headings.
Bibliography and appendices complete the report.
2) Report Writing Guide — Leeds Beckett University
Provides a textbook structure for formal reports used in academic and professional settings.
Typical sections:
Table of Contents
Abstract or Executive Summary
Introduction
Main Body
Summary/Conclusions
Recommendations
Reference List/Bibliography
Appendices
3) Business Report Structure — University of Westminster
Focuses on business reports with clear guidance on each section’s purpose.
Highlights include:
Executive Summary draft best written last, 10% of report length.
Introduction explains purpose, scope, and context.
Conclusion should summarize key findings without introducing new info.
4) Formal Report Writing Guidelines — Montgomery College
Details how to write Executive Summary as a standalone portion that previews the entire report.
Executive summary components:
Purpose
Key findings
Conclusions
Recommendations
5) Report Structuring Principles — Western Sydney University
Provides a breakdown of the parts of a business report: from cover/title page through appendices.
Standard sections:
Cover, Title Page, Table of Contents
Executive Summary/Abstract
Introduction
Findings/Discussion
Conclusions
Recommendations (optional)
Bibliography/References
Appendices

📌 Summary of Standard Report Sections
Across sources, the canonical structure looks like:
Title Page — Report title, author, date
Executive Summary / Abstract — Self-contained overview (write last)
Table of Contents — Section titles and page numbers
Introduction — Purpose, background, scope
Main Body / Discussion — Analysis, evidence, findings
Conclusion — Summary of findings, no new info
Recommendations (optional) — Practical next steps
Bibliography / References — All sources cited
Appendices — Supplementary materials
